Imagine that Mumbai has 100,000 vehicles travelling through its road network.

Instead of reducing the number of vehicles, MARG attempts to improve how those vehicles move.

It will:
1. Map the existing road network.
2. Identify congestion-prone road segments.
3. Identify bottlenecks and poorly connected areas.
4. Detect potential alternative routes.
5. Recommend new road connections and highway corridors.
6. Simulate how traffic could redistribute after those connections are added.
7. Compare travel time, network efficiency and congestion before and after the proposed infrastructure.
   
The important distinction: MARG will recommend infrastructure using a computational model. 
It will not claim that a proposed road will solve actual traffic until the recommendations are validated against real traffic counts, 
engineering constraints, land availability and environmental impact.
